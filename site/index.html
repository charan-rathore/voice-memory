01 · Was it finalized?

“Was the $42,000 revision finalized?”

Not as a final agreement. The memo that introduced $42,000 calls it a proposal until client finance signs off. Two later records say that sign-off never came.

02 · The challenge

“But wasn’t it $48,000?”

It was, in the first proposal. The September 18 revision replaced it. One proposal replacing another is still a proposal.

03 · The reason

“Why did the fee change?”

The scope shrank. Northstar asked for two regions instead of three, and the memo cut the fee “following the narrower scope”.

04 · The trap

“So the 40/60 split is settled?”

It’s open. The memo proposes 40/60, the steering notes discussed 50/50 without a vote, and the action log lists it as undecided.

Three mistakes it won’t make for you.

Newest≠agreed

$42,000 is the latest figure in the record. The memo that introduced it says they are a proposal until that sign-off is recorded, so the answer leads with that.

Discussed≠decided

The memo proposes 40/60. The steering notes discussed 50/50 and say no vote was taken. Two splits on record means the split is still open.

Pending≠rejected

Ask whether finance turned the new fee down and you hear what the documents support: approval is pending, and no rejection is recorded.

No model writes the answer.

Bolna runs the call: it hears you and speaks back. Our endpoint stands in as the agent’s model, and every sentence it returns is built from claims that carry a document, a date and a status.

Your documents only. Retrieval runs over the bundle you imported, and every quote keeps its SHA-256 hash and character offsets.

Fast enough to disappear. The engine answered each demo question in 2 to 49 ms in our local run. On a call, the wait you hear is speech, not lookup.

Drafts, never sends. A follow-up email is a draft for you to review. Nothing here can send a message by itself.
